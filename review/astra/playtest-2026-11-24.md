# Keyflame playtest · November 24, 2026

**Fix the rider-save race first.** A guest can finish a lesson, use both “Keep me” buttons, reload immediately and come back as a new guest with zero embers. A second visible contradiction affects learning: a child can see “95% Correct” and “95% correct = second star” alongside a one-star award.

## How I played

I ran the main build at 537c8c632c3aa19f037225d82deb6e844ec275ad against the repository's local PGlite/Realtime stand-in. I drove fresh Chromium game sessions using on-screen touch-key input for the tablet and phone and keyboard input for the two computers. I varied mistakes and simulated keystroke intervals and days. Each comparable route included **10 lesson attempts and nine solo races over three simulated days**. That is 76 completed activities across four riders. I kept progress earned on these routes separate from the late-game fixtures below. These are simulated players; enjoyment and likely quitting points are my interpretation of the observed play.

## What each player experienced

The starter Emberback egg was already in the nest at first launch for every rider. Times below are cumulative active typing minutes recorded by the game, rounded to one decimal. The hatch column is the first result at which the egg was ready to open. “New tribe” means receiving its first egg, which can go into the basket while another egg warms.

|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| 7 · portrait iPad · 7 WPM target | 5.8 min | 11.4 min | None by 38.9 min | 3 of 20 lessons passed, 9 races, 0 wins, 1 dragon, 146 embers |
| 9 · Android phone · 12 WPM | 3.1 min | 5.7 min | Tidefin at 16.4 min | 8 lessons passed, 5 race wins, 2 Emberbacks |
| 11 · 1366×625 laptop · 20 WPM | 1.8 min | 3.9 min | Frostspine at 5.8 min | 10 lessons passed, 8 race wins, 2 Emberbacks |
| 13 · 1920×1080 desktop · 35 WPM | 1.1 min | 2.1 min | Duneskimmer at 1.1 min | 10 lessons passed, 9 race wins, 1 dragon, 2 more tribe eggs waiting behind Duneskimmer |

- **7.** Cracking the first egg and seeing a named dragon was the strongest payoff. Missed lessons still gave warmth, so the child could hatch despite repeated failures. The likely exit point came afterward. Following the two obvious activities for nearly 39 typing minutes produced no second tribe and ended four embers short of even buying another known-tribe egg. “Go slow!” gives little help to someone already typing six measured WPM.
- **9.** Race → warm the egg → hatch worked within one short sitting. Thumb cues and a reachable pinned keyboard made mistakes recoverable. Seeing a second Emberback before the Tidefin could feel repetitive if the player missed the basket. The Nest is where that choice becomes understandable.
- **11.** The entire guide keyboard fit the short laptop viewport, ending at y=617 of 625. Home, Nest and Race again on results kept replay quick. An impatient player can overlook a new achievement while racing again. A named reward is easier to recognize later than a count of new achievements.
- **13.** The regular first course paid out rapidly. The starter-course path produced a win in all nine races and several tribe eggs, while the current Duneskimmer still needed 1,909 more keys. The player has two distinct goals here: a harder race today and a long-term collection. Help them choose the harder course as soon as they qualify.

In an additional desktop run I picked the hardest unlocked course and played three sessions of roughly six simulated typing minutes each. The rider reached Starfall Caves and raced on a cat-fact passage. At 19.8 active minutes the Duneskimmer egg stood at 2,918/3,000 keys, with five other tribe eggs waiting. Harder races and varied text are already there; surfacing them on results would help the challenge-seeking player find them.

## Checks worth keeping

A teacher created a class, printed the three badge cards, and started a race with three joined riders. All three saw two classmates and reached their results screen. I separately created a family wing from the grown-up Family view and joined it with its code, badge and secret word. The Ideas flow stayed local.

The four sampled race screens had no horizontal document overflow. The on-screen keyboard occupied y=925–1180 on the tablet and y=596–844 on the phone. I saw no input freeze on the completed routes. In a separate ordinary-clock perfect F and J lesson, the live counter stopped at 19/20 rings and stayed there after the result appeared. Refresh the counter on the finishing key before clearing the 250 ms update timer. These were Chromium viewport simulations; native Safari behavior and device CPU/battery performance remain unmeasured.

The later-game fixture let me complete Daily Flight's Animals word challenge, finish a warm-up followed by T and Y, open the Codex, wear a title, rename a hatched Tidefin and place the Moss rug. A second Tidefin was moved from the basket into the nest and warmed with embers. With matching hatch randomness it evolved the existing named dragon to one star. That fixture verifies the interactions separately from time-to-reward measurements.

Finger and thumb cues, targeted practice and returning older keys in warm-ups are observable teaching supports. The scores reflect the input rates supplied to the simulation. Measuring actual improvement would require a child typing unfamiliar text without cues before and after practice.
